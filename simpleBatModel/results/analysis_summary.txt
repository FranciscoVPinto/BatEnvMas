Interpretation:
  - For fixed-alpha scenarios (pv01–04), the degradation penalty does NOT change
    the grid import cost at all, because alpha is fixed a priori. The optimizer
    cannot reduce battery cycling to save cost — battery dispatch is determined by
    the fixed alpha and must meet that share allocation.
  - Only for pv09 (optimal alpha), where alpha itself is a decision variable, does
    the degradation penalty cause any behavioural change. With α=0.033 EUR/kWh
    the solver slightly reduces battery throughput (dis: 86.2→83.8 kWh), accepting
    marginally more imports instead, raising net cost by only +0.59 EUR over 28 days.
  - Over a full year the effect remains very small (+0.08 EUR for pv09). At
    α=0.033 EUR/kWh the degradation cost for pv09_1year ≈
    (5002.4 kWh import − same) implies battery throughput of ≈ few hundred kWh/year,
    costing only a few EUR in objective — the model already minimises cycling.
  - ANOMALY: The near-zero impact of the linear degradation penalty across all
    scenarios suggests either (a) the battery throughput in these scenarios is
    already close to the minimum necessary to satisfy load, or (b) α=0.033 EUR/kWh
    is too low relative to c_grid=0.27 EUR/kWh for the penalty to discourage cycling
    significantly. The PWL formulation (pv15–19) was introduced to address this.

--- 5.3  PWL SOLVER FAILURES ---

  All pv15–19 cases at 3months and 6months returned infeasible/no solution.
  pv16, pv18, pv19 even at 1month are infeasible.
  This is a significant finding: the PWL MIP formulation with binary bin-selection
  variables has scalability issues. Possible causes:
    (a) The cyclic SoC constraint combined with binary PWL bin variables creates
        a numerically difficult MIP. The solver cannot find any feasible solution
        within the time limit for larger horizons.
    (b) The appsi_highs interface may have parameter settings different from the
        standard highs interface used for pv01–14.
    (c) Some model configurations (pv16=weighted, pv18=mean, pv19=optimal) may
        have tighter alpha constraints that cause infeasibility when combined with
        the PWL degradation binary constraints.
  RECOMMENDATION: For longer horizons, reformulate PWL as a continuous convex
  approximation or use a rolling-horizon decomposition.

Key observations:
  - pv09 (optimal) achieves the highest self-consumption (69.6%) and lowest

    by maximising self-use of PV through optimal battery dispatch.
  - pv12 (nothing: no battery, no sharing, but export allowed) wastes 73.3% of
    PV as export at c_sell=0.06 EUR/kWh while importing at c_grid=0.27 EUR/kWh.
  - pv10 (no battery) also exports 69.5% — the battery is crucial for self-consumption.
  - Export always gives zero curtailment; no-export forces curtailment of exactly
    the same volume that would have been exported, confirming model consistency.
  - Total PV generation (12573 kWh/year) exceeds total load (13310 kWh/year) by
    only 5.7% deficit, meaning battery storage + export can nearly offset all
    imports. pv09 achieves 62.4% SSR, leaving 37.6% of load still grid-imported.

================================================================================
SECTION 8: ANOMALIES AND NOTES
================================================================================

1. DEGRADATION IMPACT NEAR-ZERO FOR FIXED-ALPHA CASES (pv01–04, pv14):
   With α=0.033 EUR/kWh the model cannot change battery dispatch for fixed-alpha
   scenarios because the alpha allocation already determines battery scheduling.
   The degradation term in the objective is numerically non-zero but the optimal
   solution is identical. This confirms that linear degradation only affects cases
   where the optimizer has freedom over battery use (pv09/pv13).

2. pv02 vs pv14 IDENTICAL RESULTS:
   pv14 (weighted + degradation) gives exactly the same net cost and SSR as pv02
   (weighted, no degradation) at ALL horizons. The weighted-alpha is fixed, so the
   battery dispatch is pinned by the alpha allocation — degradation penalty is
   absorbed into the objective value but changes no decisions.

3. pv09 1YEAR DEBUG FILE:
   The presence of debug_infeasibility.txt in the pv09_1year directory alongside a
   valid optimal meta.yaml and CSVs is an artefact of a prior aborted solver run.
   The current results are confirmed optimal.

4. PWL SOLVER SCALABILITY FAILURE:
   13 of 15 PWL cases failed entirely. The PWL MIP binary variables make the
   problem NP-hard in a practical sense for horizons ≥ 3months. Even the two
   successful 1month cases (pv15, pv17) only produced time-limit incumbents.
   The PWL degradation model as implemented cannot scale to the full horizon study.

5. pv15 HIGH-THROUGHPUT APARTMENTS (1month):
   Apt1, Apt4, Apt7 in pv15 spend ~99.8% of time in bin_2 (highest degradation
   bin). This is physically unrealistic and may indicate a model issue: the equal
   alpha forces these apartments to cycle their battery far more than neighbours,
   accumulating very high degradation costs (3.90 EUR, 1.82 EUR, 2.31 EUR in just
   one month ≈ 47 EUR, 22 EUR, 28 EUR annualised).

6. pv12 (NOTHING) PARADOX:
   Despite having allow_export=True, pv12 (no battery, no sharing) exports the
   most PV (73.3%) at a low sell rate while importing at a high buy rate. Net cost
   is 2133 EUR/year — the worst of all scenarios. The export revenue (9210 kWh ×
   0.06 ≈ 553 EUR) does not compensate for high import costs (9947 kWh × ~0.23 avg
   ≈ ~2286 EUR gross). This strongly justifies the shared battery investment.

7. 6MONTHS NET COST LOWER THAN 3MONTHS:
   pv09: 3months=338.90 EUR vs 6months=190.38 EUR. This appears counterintuitive
   (longer period, lower cost). Reason: 6months covers Jan–Jun 2025, which includes
   spring/summer PV surplus. The export revenue during Apr–Jun exceeds import costs
   for many hours, making the 6-month net cost (grid cost minus export revenue)
   lower than the 3-month winter-dominant cost.

8. NO-EXPORT COSTS ALWAYS HIGHER:
   At every horizon, no-export variants cost more than their export counterparts.
   The cost premium grows strongly with PV availability:
     1month (low PV): pv03 vs pv07 ≈ 0.00 EUR difference
     3months:         pv03 vs pv07 = 344.78 vs 357.51 = +12.73 EUR
     6months:         pv03 vs pv07 = 203.54 vs 367.50 = +163.96 EUR
     1year:           pv03 vs pv07 = 1149.09 vs 1387.84 = +238.75 EUR
